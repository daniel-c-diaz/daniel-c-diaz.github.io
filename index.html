I am a computational research scientist at the San Diego Supercomputer Center, UC San Diego, and a member of the CMS Collaboration at CERN.

My research looks for particles that live long enough to travel measurably through a detector before they decay — a signature predicted by many dark sector models, and one that conventional LHC searches are built to discard. I have led CMS searches for long-lived particles decaying in the tracker and in the endcap muon detectors, and I develop machine-learning algorithms that run inside the Level-1 trigger so that these events are kept in the first place.

Before joining SDSC I was a postdoctoral scholar at UC San Diego with Javier Duarte, and an LHC Physics Center Distinguished Researcher at Fermilab in 2023 and 2024. I completed my Ph.D. at Florida State University in 2020.

Research

  • Searches for long-lived particles and dark sectors. Higgs-portal and low-mass mediators decaying to displaced jets in the tracker, and to showers in the endcap muon detectors — including a search on the CMS B-parking dataset.
  • Machine learning in the CMS Level-1 trigger. Displaced-jet taggers and learned missing-energy algorithms deployed on FPGAs, with hls4ml, inside a microsecond latency budget.
  • Di-Higgs searches and future colliders. Boosted HH → bbbb and bbVV in Runs 2 and 3; ML-based jet tagging for di-Higgs sensitivity at the FCC-hh.
  • FAIR practice and AI infrastructure. Community standards for findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable AI models in high energy physics; large-scale research computing at SDSC.
